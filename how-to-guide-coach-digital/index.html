How-to guide: Coach Digital

How to Implement Coach Digital in Your Context

Who this guide is for

This guide is for ministries, implementation partners, school leaders, and coaches bringing Coach Digital into an existing teacher professional development program. It combines a rollout plan with the documented classroom workflow. A technical team is needed to deploy and maintain the system; coaches can use the steps below once their local implementation is ready.

Before you start

  • Define the coaching model: Agree who coaches whom, the priority teaching practices, the observation rubric, and how feedback and follow-up will work.
  • Confirm readiness: Check Android devices, charging, connectivity, coach digital confidence, and access to local support. Test the actual application build on devices you will use.
  • Agree on data stewardship: Define access permissions, data ownership, retention, and how teacher information and any photographs will be handled.
  • Appoint owners: Name a program lead, a pedagogical lead, a technical maintainer, and a person responsible for helping coaches.

Step-by-step plan

Step 1 — Adapt and test the system

What to do: Use the system overview and deployment guides to configure the mobile app, backend, and administration dashboard. Translate labels and resources, set up teaching practices and questions, and test a full observation-to-feedback cycle with local users. Include a test without internet and a later synchronization test.

Why it matters: The app needs to reflect your coaching program and work in the conditions coaches actually face.

Step 2 — Prepare schools and devices

What to do: In the dashboard, add or import schools and check names, EMIS identifiers, and locations. Generate each school’s QR code. Provide coaches with the approved Android installation package and instructions from your administrator. With a connection available, scan the school QR code, let its data load, and select or create the coach profile. Add other assigned schools through “Switch Schools.”

Why it matters: Correct setup connects observations to the right school and teacher, and loads the information needed for field use.

Step 3 — Prepare and observe a lesson

What to do: Select the teacher and choose “New Class Observation,” or use “Start New Session” from the home screen. Follow the preparation prompts, complete “About the Lesson” together with the teacher, and observe the agreed teaching practices. Use the configured rubric to rate micro-teaching practices, then review and confirm the observation summary.

Why it matters: Shared preparation establishes the purpose of the visit. Consistent ratings and concrete classroom evidence give the feedback conversation a clear foundation.

Step 4 — Give feedback and agree on one focus

What to do: Begin the coaching session after the observation. Select one micro-teaching practice for improvement, discuss what happened in the lesson, and agree on an action the teacher can practice. Record the conversation and next steps as typed feedback or a photo of handwritten notes, following your data rules. If feedback must wait, return through the teacher’s incomplete session or pending sessions list.

Why it matters: Observation alone does not complete the coaching cycle. One manageable focus helps the teacher turn feedback into practice.

Step 5 — Synchronize and follow up

What to do: When connectivity is available, allow the device to synchronize and check that the records reach the dashboard. Before the next visit, review previous ratings, feedback, and agreed actions. Repeat the cycle and revisit the teacher’s progress. In the documented app, teacher statistics appear after at least three completed coaching sessions.

Why it matters: Coaching becomes ongoing support when each visit builds on the last. Synchronization makes field activity visible to program teams.

Step 6 — Use program data to improve support

What to do: Filter the dashboard by area and time period. Review teachers reached, active coaches, repeat sessions, and observations awaiting feedback. Examine teaching practices targeted for improvement, export data where needed, and use the Sync Log to investigate stale records. Combine these records with conversations and quality checks.

Why it matters: Program teams can identify gaps in coverage and support. Counts and ratings need interpretation before they inform decisions about teaching quality.

Timeline examples

Plan a staged rollout: local adaptation and setup, a small field pilot, revisions and training, then expansion. The guidance note describes approximately six months of development for the earlier Mozambique pilot; that is historical context, not a schedule for Sierra Leone, Nepal, or your implementation. Set milestones for successful device setup, completed feedback cycles, reliable synchronization, and a supported handover before expanding.

Roles and responsibilities

  • Program owner: Sets priorities, secures resources, and approves rollout and data use.
  • Pedagogical lead: Adapts the coaching protocol, trains coaches, and checks observation and feedback quality.
  • Coaches and school leaders: Prepare visits, observe lessons, give feedback, and maintain accurate records.
  • Technical team: Deploys and updates the software, manages hosting and backups, and resolves device and synchronization issues.
  • Monitoring team: Reviews coverage, data completeness, and progress, and coordinates evaluation.

Common challenges and practical fixes

  • No connection during a visit: Complete initial school setup while connected and test offline use before rollout. Plan a reliable opportunity to synchronize afterward.
  • Observation recorded but feedback missing: Reopen the teacher’s incomplete session, review the observation, and complete the coaching conversation.
  • Dashboard figures look out of date: Check the selected region and date range, then review device synchronization and the Sync Log. Unsynchronized activity will not yet appear centrally.
  • New questions are not visible on devices: Coaches need a connection to synchronize questionnaire changes. Coordinate changes with training and the reporting team.
  • Scores differ between coaches: Practice rating the same lesson examples together and discuss differences using the agreed rubric.

Success metrics

  • Coverage: Schools and teachers reached, including those in remote locations.
  • Completed support: Share of observations followed by feedback; repeat coaching sessions; time between visits.
  • Reliability: Successful synchronization, missing records, and time to resolve support requests.
  • Quality: Consistency of observation ratings, usefulness of feedback, and follow-through on agreed actions.
  • Outcomes: Changes in independently assessed teaching practice and, where evaluated, student learning. App activity alone is not an outcome measure.
